QUEUE-ARCHIVE + APPROVED-LIST / Publication 8 / 2026-11-11 / awaiting your tweaks

Paid requests move to monthly archives; unlisted requesters wait for the board

Two queued changes to the reimbursement queue. The first lets the ordinary uv run np-finance end-to-end-run prepare receipts again by moving paid requests' receipts onto one archive page per submission month. The second reads your Approved Reimbursees tab on every refresh and holds requests from anyone who is not on it for board review.

What I heard

Queue archive

  • The queue page has reached its 100 MiB receipt budget, so the ordinary run stops at receipts_failed. Paid requests use 94.1 MiB of it.
  • Keep open requests and email follow-ups in the working queue, and move paid requests to archives.
  • Group the archives by the month a request was submitted, not the month it was paid, because that is what people remember. One page per month.

Approved list

  • The board wants oversight of who is reimbursed. Anyone on the approved list can be marked Approved straight away; anyone else goes to Clarification for board scrutiny, even when the purchases look fine.
  • Make it automatic: "that's why we are doing this."
  • Near matches do not count. A similar name with a different email stays in Clarification.
  • Requests from people on the list that you already approved stay approved, and a request can still be approved after the board reviews it.

Why this shape

Paid requests are history. They rarely need their receipts open in the working queue, but they must stay findable. Submission-month pages keep every file well inside its own budget (the busiest month is about 32 MiB), and a text row in the working queue keeps every paid request searchable.

The approved-list gate sits in the refresh, which already decides each request's advice. The two commands that refresh read the tab read-only and record the result in the private bundle, so report-reimbursements can re-render offline and reach the same answer. Your recorded reviews still win: the gate changes only advice the toolkit produced on its own.
